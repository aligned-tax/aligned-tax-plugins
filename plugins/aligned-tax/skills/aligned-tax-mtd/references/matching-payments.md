# When a payment does not match

Used by [guides/folder.md](../guides/folder.md), [adding-transactions.md](adding-transactions.md) and for marketplace payouts and invoices. Every step follows the seven rules in SKILL.md.

A bank payment and the records behind it often differ for ordinary reasons. Look at the records first (the invoices, the payout report, the bank line, each with its date and reference); ask the person to explain only what the records cannot.

## Why a payment may not match

- **Bulk or part payments.** One payment covers several invoices or sales, or an invoice is paid in parts.
- **Fees.** A marketplace, card processor or letting agent keeps a fee before paying out.
- **Credits and refunds.** A refund or credit note reduces what is paid.
- **Reversals.** A payment was taken back, or paid twice and returned.
- **Settlement timing.** The sale and the money arrive on different dates, sometimes in different tax years.
- **Transfers between the person's own accounts.** These are never income or expenses.
- **Currency.** The payout is in another currency; never convert it (see "Needs you" in [guides/folder.md](../guides/folder.md)).

## How matches work

- A match can involve several records on either side: many sales to one payout, or one invoice to several payments.
- One payment can cover several invoices, but the amounts allocated to them never add up to more than the payment.
- Never state a computed difference as a fact (rule 1). Say which records you looked at and what they print, and that the printed figures do not agree; the amounts come from the records, not from your sum. This is the same care as the letting agent check in [splitting-and-duplicates.md](splitting-and-duplicates.md).
- A record you cannot match goes under "Still needs you" with what you checked. Never invent a fee, a date or an allocation to make it fit.
